# Human pancreas: tissue architecture

Below about 0.1 mm there are no Visible Human data, so every tissue level is a model built from published measurements, marked `simulated`, never a picture of this person's tissue. `src/domain/human-pancreas-tissue.ts` lists the levels and their status; each built model has a manifest giving every parameter with its source, or saying it has none yet, and every result beside the target it was meant to meet.

## Sources

HRA provides three 2D functional tissue units for the pancreas (islet, acinus, intercalated duct; ccf-releases v2.0, CC BY 4.0), stylized illustrations of average cell populations, and the ASCT+B table with Cell Ontology terms. The quantitative primary sources, checked on PubMed, are these. Hahn et al. 2021 (doi:10.1038/s42003-021-02589-x) imaged whole human pancreata in 3D: endocrine tissue about 1–2% of the gland; mean islet 3D diameter 110 µm over 197,097 islets; higher islet volume density in the tail; dense islet clusters at the periphery holding about 40% of islets. Wang et al. 2013 (doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0067454): islet density similar in head and body, more than twice as high in the tail, composition and architecture similar throughout. Cabrera et al. 2006 (doi:10.1073/pnas.0510790103): no subdivisions in human islets, cells along vessels in no particular order, 71% of beta cells in contact with other endocrine cells. Brissova et al. 2005 (doi:10.1369/jhc.5C6684.2005): cell types dispersed throughout, composition varying considerably between islets. Poudel et al. 2016 (doi:10.1038/srep34049): whole-pancreas stereology.

## The representative islet

`scripts/human/build_islet.py` builds one islet of the mean diameter, 110 µm. Cells are packed as solid tissue, a jittered face-centred cubic lattice in random orientation. Capillaries are added until 95% of the remaining cells lie within 1.5 cell diameters of one; cells they cut are removed. Types are assigned in the proportions of the HRA islet unit (54% beta, 31% alpha, 9% delta, 6% PP). The share of beta cells touching another type is then measured as Cabrera et al. did, in sections: five parallel sections of the model, profiles touching within 15% of contact.

Result: 346 cells, 17 capillaries; composition within 0.2 percentage points of the target; 71% ± 4% of 121 beta cells in the sections touch another type, against 71% measured. Random assignment already meets the measurement, so the model adds no clustering: Cabrera's figure is consistent with near-random intermixing in human islets. Single sections vary widely (69% at the centre, 86% at 10 µm above it), which is why the calibration pools five.

Not yet sourced, and marked so: cell diameter (11 µm, which sets the number of cells), capillary radius (3 µm), and the capillary reach rule. Epsilon cells are omitted, as in the HRA unit. Cells are drawn as spheres for clarity; in tissue they are polyhedral. The islet is one representative; real islets vary in size, shape and composition.

## The islet distribution

`scripts/human/build_islet_distribution.py` places islets through the HRA organ. The region meshes are open where they meet each other, so they have no volume of their own; the union of the five closes, and is voxelized at 0.5 mm and filled. Each inside voxel goes to the region whose surface is nearest. The organs come out at 77.2 cm³ (male) and 84.1 cm³ (female), surface voxels counted half; Hahn et al. give about 73 cm³ on average, so the HRA organs are of human size.

The number of islets is the endocrine fraction (1.5%, the middle of Hahn's 1–2%) times the organ volume, divided by the volume of a 110 µm islet: 1.66 million (male) and 1.81 million (female), 21.5 per mm³ on average. This overestimates a little, because islet sizes are skewed. Density is the same in head, uncinate process, neck and body and twice as high in the tail (Wang et al. report more than 2-fold; 2.0 is the low end; neck and uncinate are not reported). 40% of islets lie in clusters near the surface at three times the region's mean density (Hahn). Cluster sizes (100 to 400 islets) and the depth counted as periphery (3 mm) are assumptions and are marked so. One point is stored per 100 islets.

The tests check, in both bodies, the tail-to-head-and-body density ratio, the clustered share, the islet count against the formula, an organ volume of human size, and that every point lies inside the organ.

## The tissue block: acini, centroacinar cells, ducts, capillaries, stroma and an islet

`scripts/human/build_tissue_block.py` builds a 300 µm block from inside a lobule and writes its layout to `public/atlas-human/tissue-block.json`; a viewer builds each cell's shape from the parameters there.

Ducts and acini. An intralobular duct enters on one face and ramifies by space colonization, tapering by Murray's law. Acini bud from it in grape-like clusters (CODA, Kiemen et al. 2022, doi:10.1038/s41592-022-01650-9), each on its own short intercalated duct, kept only if it touches nothing already placed; a second pass scans a 6 µm grid for remaining pockets, largest first, and fills each with the largest acinus that fits on a clear duct. Each acinus has one path to the intralobular duct and acini never join one another (Endo et al. 2002, doi:10.14670/HH-17.107, in rat). Acini are rounded, 13 to 19 µm in radius; their cells are smooth wedges converging on a small central lumen like orange segments, purple at the base and pink at the apex, with a round basal nucleus and zymogen granules about 1 µm across in the apical third (Messenger et al. 2014, doi:10.1016/j.ceca.2014.03.003; rat measurements by Nadelhaft 1973 and Ermak and Rothman 1981). Centroacinar cells, pale and flattened with elongated nuclei, line the intra-acinar duct from the mouth to the lumen (Endo et al.; Beer et al. 2016, doi:10.1016/j.ydbio.2016.02.027).

Capillaries and stroma. Capillaries form a looping network in the gaps between acini: points in free space near acinar surfaces are joined when the segment between them stays clear of every acinus, duct and the islet, keeping a minimum spanning tree plus the shortest extra links. Red cells are drawn in single file. The islet keeps its own dense capillary bed, recovered from the islet model. Collagen fibres run along the intralobular ducts, aligned with them as CODA found; loose fibres and spindle-shaped stellate cells lie between acini.

Result: 399 acini, 13,186 acinar cells, 1,197 centroacinar cells, the islet's 346 cells, 18,224 capillary segments (and 812 in the islet), every acinus within 6 µm of a capillary, 2,740 collagen fibres and 140 stellate cells.

Deformation and packing. Real acini press against one another and round the vessels between them. After the network is built, each acinar cell grows outward from its acinus's sphere, along its own direction, until it reaches the territory of a neighbouring acinus (a power diagram, which divides space between spheres of different sizes), a capillary, a duct, the islet or the edge of the block, stopping 0.5 µm short and never beyond twice the radius. Each acinus stores these reaches (`ext`), one per cell. Measured by 120,000 random points, acinar cells fill 52% of the block, against 26% for the same acini as rigid spheres; the rest is capillaries, ducts, stroma, the islet and small gaps between wedge-shaped cells. An earlier version reported 66% from overlapping spheres, a bug the tests now prevent: they forbid overlap, require every reach between the radius and twice the radius, and fail if packing falls below 45%.

Not yet sourced, and marked so: beta cell and insulin granule size and number; acinar cell height and base, acinar nucleus, acinus radius and lumen, centroacinar size and number, intra-acinar duct length, duct radii and cell heights, capillary radius and reach, red cell and stellate cell sizes.

## From organ to cell

`pancreas-organ-to-cell.html` (published) is one continuous zoom. Everything lives in one world measured in micrometres: the point clicked on the HRA organ is the origin, the tissue block is centred there, and each finer level sits inside the coarser one at true scale, so the acinar cell drawn in detail is a real cell of a real acinus in the block, the beta cell a real cell of the islet, and the duct cell a real cell of an intralobular duct. Levels fade in and out with the camera's distance (organ above about 4 mm, the block from about 16 mm down to 0.2 mm, the cut-open structure from 0.4 mm down to about 25 µm, the cell below about 50 µm), and the camera's target drifts from the block's centre to the chosen structure and then to its cell as one zooms, so the whole journey spans six orders of magnitude without a cut. Each level covers about a tenfold range of zoom before the next takes over. Below the tissue there are three branches, each in three stages. First the chosen structure on its own, without its neighbours: one acinus with its centroacinar cells, its own intercalated duct and the ducts it drains into, back toward the intralobular duct; the islet with its capillaries; or an intralobular duct with the segments joined to it. A cutaway faces the viewer through the point being zoomed into (a duct is opened lengthwise). Zooming on, its cells separate: the acinus's wedge-shaped cells ease out along their own axes like the segments of an orange, each carrying its nucleus and granules; the islet's cells draw apart from its centre; the duct's cells lift off the wall and spread along it. Zooming further, the others fade and one cell stands alone in full detail: an acinar cell, a beta cell or a duct cell. Zooming out reverses each step. The zoom distances of each stage are scaled to the structure's size, so the islet, three times the size of an acinus, separates further out.

The duct cell is cuboidal, with a central nucleus, many mitochondria, apical microvilli, one primary cilium (in the pancreas only islet and duct cells have one; diIorio et al. 2014, doi:10.1002/bdrc.21064), a tight-junction belt round its apex and a basement membrane beneath. CFTR and the Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger SLC26A6 are marked in its apical membrane, where they carry bicarbonate into the lumen (Steward and Ishiguro 2009, doi:10.1097/MOG.0b013e32832e06ce); duct cells secrete the bicarbonate-rich fluid of pancreatic juice, 2 to 3 litres a day, at up to 140 to 150 mM bicarbonate (Ishiguro et al. 2012, PMC4831246; Hegyi and Rakonczay 2015, doi:10.1016/j.pan.2015.03.010). Duct cells in the block are drawn as smooth rounded boxes. The acinar cell shows the secretory pathway (Palade 1975, doi:10.1126/science.1096303); the beta cell its insulin granules as dark crystalline cores of insulin with zinc (Berclaz et al. 2016, doi:10.1007/s00125-015-3819-x) in pale halos, beside a capillary. Organelle, transporter and granule sizes and numbers are illustrative. The block is generic: it is the same wherever on the organ one zooms in.

## Regions and the gross view

There is one tissue block per region (`tissue-block.json` for the body, `tissue-block-head.json`, `tissue-block-tail.json`), each built independently by `scripts/human/build_tissue_block.py <region>` with its own seed, so acini, ducts, vessels and the islet's position differ; all meet the same rules, which the tests check for each. Every block holds one islet: at the model's densities a 300 µm block holds about half an islet in the head and body and about one in the tail, so a single block cannot show the regional difference.

That difference is shown at the gross level, a 4 mm piece of each region between the organ and the block: lobules of acinar tissue (Voronoi cells of jittered seeds, about 1.25 mm apart, shrunk by half a 50 µm septum; both values typical, not yet sourced), interlobular ducts with an artery and a vein in the septa, and islets at the region's density in the islet distribution model (17 per mm³ in head and body, 34 in the tail, after Wang et al. 2013 and Hahn et al. 2021), sized lognormally about the 110 µm mean. The viewer can set the three pieces side by side. The 40% of islets in dense peripheral clusters (Hahn) are not drawn here, since each piece is taken from inside the gland. In the viewer the uncinate process takes the head's block and the neck the body's.

## Many models: sixty tissue sites, and acini, ducts and islets to choose from

`src/domain/human-pancreas-generator.ts` generates a tissue block or an islet from a seed, by the same rules as the Python builders, so that many can be made where they are needed instead of being stored (each Python block is about 1 MB and three minutes to build). Tests generate sample blocks and check every rule the builder's blocks meet: one intercalated duct per acinus ending at it, no acini overlapping or inside the islet, every cell's reach between its acinus's radius and twice that radius, packing above 45%, a capillary near every acinus, and the same output for the same seed. A block takes about five seconds.

Each generated block makes its own islet first, so space is kept for it. Islets vary as islets do: diameters lognormal about the 110 µm mean (Hahn et al. 2021; the spread, sigma 0.3, is assumed), ellipsoids with axes up to 15% from round (assumed), and composition drawn about the HRA average with a spread that is assumed but reflects Brissova et al. 2005, who found it varies considerably between islets. Types are assigned at random, as random intermixing matches Cabrera's measurement. Tests check that 25 islets average 110 µm within 15 µm, that sizes and beta-cell shares vary, and that every cell lies inside its islet.

The viewer has sixty tissue sites, twenty each in the head (with the uncinate process), the body (with the neck) and the tail, shown as dots on the organ. The first in each region is the block built and tested in the repo; the others are spread through the region and generated in the browser from their seeds when one zooms in. Each site has its own 4 mm gross piece, its own block and its own islet, so there are sixty islets to compare. Within a block, twenty-five acini and twenty-five intralobular ducts are chosen by farthest-point sampling so that they are spread out and differ, and arrows step through them; for the islet, the arrows step through the sites.

## Viewer tools

Following the features of MouseBio_3D (its Play journey, names on the model, and selection tools that isolate a structure or change its opacity), the zoom viewer has:

- Slice apart: a knob that cuts the organ into eight transverse slices, or the tissue block into six serial sections, and spreads them apart, as serial sections are spread for 3D reconstruction (CODA). Each slab draws only the cells near it, so slicing costs about as much as the whole block. Labels move with their slab.
- Play the journey: an automatic tour from the organ through an acinus and acinar cell, a duct and duct cell, and the islet and a beta cell, and back, with captions. Any scroll, click or key stops it.
- Names: labels on the organ's regions, ducts and vessels; on the acinus, islet, duct, capillary and stellate cell in the tissue; on the parts of each cut-open structure; and on the organelles of each cell.
- Click to identify: clicking any cell or structure in the tissue names and describes it; Isolate shows only that kind, and an opacity slider fades it.
- Split screen: two views side by side, each with its own camera, zoom stage and structure, drawn from the same scene; the panel controls the view last touched. Presets set up an islet beside an acinus (endocrine and exocrine), an islet beside a duct, an acinus beside a duct, a beta cell beside an acinar cell, the tissue beside a cell, or the organ beside the tissue.
- Narration: the journey speaks each step with the browser's own voice (Web Speech API) while the camera moves, and goes on as soon as the line is finished; without a voice, each caption stays up for as long as it takes to read. Flights and stage changes are quicker than before.
- Graphics: soft image-based light from a studio room (three.js RoomEnvironment) with filmic tone mapping, kept as a fill under directional light; a velvety sheen on cells and a glossy coat on granules and nuclei in the close-up stages; smoother, higher-resolution cell shapes there; and a small variation in colour from cell to cell.
- Motion: red blood cells flow along their capillaries. The speed is illustrative, not measured; motion is off for viewers who ask for reduced motion.
