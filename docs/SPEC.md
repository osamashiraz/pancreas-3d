# Specification — Human pancreas 3D, from organ to cell

## 1. Purpose

A source-grounded, explorable 3D model of the human pancreas for teaching and research communication, built as a module of MouseBio_3D and as a standalone zoom viewer. A user can move continuously from the whole organ (about 15 cm) to a 4 mm piece of tissue, a 300 µm block, a single acinus, islet or duct, their separated cells, and one cell with its organelles, at true scale, in H&E or cell-type colours, with narration. Every element states what it rests on: drawn from the Visible Human (via HRA), or simulated by a written rule from published measurements.

## 2. Architecture

- **Repo module** (`repo/`, installed into MouseBio_3D): TypeScript domain data (inventory, circuits, tissue levels, generators), a loader using MouseBio_3D's checksum-bounded fetch, tests, Python builders, and model data under `public/atlas-human/`.
- **Standalone viewer** (`viewer/`, built to `dist/pancreas-organ-to-cell.html`): one self-contained HTML page (about 8 MB, under the 16 MB publishing limit) with three.js r147 from jsDelivr, the region model, islet distribution and three tested tissue blocks embedded, ; blocks are no longer generated in the browser (that froze the page for seconds), though the TypeScript generator and its compiled `viewer/src/generator.js` remain.
- **World**: one coordinate system in micrometres. The chosen tissue site in the organ is the origin; the tissue block is centred there; finer levels sit inside coarser ones at true scale.

## 3. Organ level (`src/domain/human-pancreas-structures.ts`)

- Source: HRA 3D Reference Object Library v2.0 (CC BY 4.0), male and female, from the Visible Human Project. `scripts/human/extract_pancreas_region.py` copies 29 named nodes per sex unchanged from the united body models into `pancreas-region-<sex>.glb` (positions verified by per-node SHA-256).
- Inventory: 38 structures per sex. Ready: 22 drawn by HRA (head, uncinate, neck, body, tail; main and accessory ducts, bile duct, ampulla, sphincter; coeliac trunk, common and proper hepatic, splenic and superior mesenteric arteries; splenic, superior mesenteric, portal and inferior pancreaticoduodenal veins; duodenum, duodenal bulb, spleen), 6 simulated vessel structures (gastroduodenal artery; superior and inferior pancreaticoduodenal arteries forming anterior and posterior arcades; dorsal and transverse, great and caudal pancreatic arteries), 4 simulated lymph node groups (21 nodes per body; Japan Pancreas Society stations 8a, 8p, 10, 11p, 11d, 13a, 13b, 14, 17a, 17b). Planned: minor papilla, superior pancreaticoduodenal veins, four nerve structures.
- Simulated vessels (`place_simulated.py`): landmarks defined by written rules (recorded with their points in `pancreas-simulated.manifest.json`); each leg is the cheapest path through a 0.75 mm grid that forbids entering any drawn structure and prefers lying radius + 0.6 mm off the followed surface; smoothed; tubes with parallel-transport frames. Tests: no contacts, no step over 1 mm, no turn over 75°, arcades close.
- Lymph nodes: per-station anchor rules and preferred side; each node at the nearest free spot; deviations recorded.
- Circuits (`human-pancreas-circuits.ts`): 14 schematic routes for supply, drainage, lymph and nerves.
- Ontology (`data/human-pancreas-ontology-candidates.json`): UBERON/FMA/CL terms copied from HRA; known errors listed (male SMV mapped to the SMA term; papilla of Santorini given the accessory duct's term; malformed FMA id; misspelt node names).

## 4. Islet distribution and gross level

- `build_islet_distribution.py`: organ volume from the voxelized union of the five regions (77.2 cm³ male, 84.1 cm³ female; Hahn et al. give about 73 cm³); islet count = 1.5% endocrine fraction × volume ÷ volume of a 110 µm islet (1.66 and 1.81 million); density in tail twice head and body (Wang et al. 2013); 40% in dense peripheral clusters at three times the density (Hahn et al. 2021). Assumed: cluster sizes 100–400, periphery depth 3 mm.
- Lobule level (viewer), opened in place: at each site a line goes into the gland (of 160 directions, the shortest way out to the surface, leaning towards the front of the body). Along it stands a column of tissue, 5 mm wide, from 2.5 mm below the site up to the surface: Voronoi lobules (seeds about 1.25 mm apart, 50 µm septa; assumed), solid, with connective tissue in the septa. Within 1.4 mm of the line and 2 mm of the site, the same lobules are also drawn as their duct tree: interlobular ducts along the minimum spanning tree of septal points, each segment bent onto the septum between the two nearest lobules and smoothed (Chaikin), the longest path being the large interlobular duct (80 µm radius) with an artery and vein beside it; an intralobular duct from the nearest point of that network to each lobule's centre, branching (6-8 primary branches) into terminal (intercalated) ducts (4-6 each) ending in clusters of acini (drawn sparser than real so the ducts show); islets at the region's density with lognormal sizes about 110 µm. Calibres assumed: interlobular 26-45 µm, intralobular 11-14 µm, terminal 7 µm radius. The comparison sets the head, body and tail columns side by side, each opened round its centre.

## 5. Tissue block (300 µm)

- Python builder `build_tissue_block.py <region>` (tested blocks for body, head, tail) and TypeScript generator `generateBlock(seed, region)` (any seed; about 5 s in the browser), same rules:
  - intralobular duct tree by space colonization, tapering by Murray's law;
  - acini (13–19 µm radius) budding in grape-like clusters (CODA) on their own intercalated ducts, then a grid pass filling remaining pockets; no anastomoses (Endo 2002, rat);
  - capillaries: minimum spanning tree plus loops through free space; every acinus within 6 µm;
  - stroma: collagen aligned along ducts (CODA), interstitial fibres, stellate cells;
  - deformation: each acinar cell reaches out until a neighbouring acinus's power-diagram territory, a capillary, a duct, the islet or the edge (packing about 52%);
  - one islet per block.
- Islets: `build_islet.py` (representative, 110 µm, 346 cells, HRA composition, Cabrera's 71% heterotypic contact met by random intermixing) and `generateIslet(seed)` (lognormal size about 110 µm, sigma 0.3 assumed; composition drawn about HRA's average with an assumed spread, reflecting Brissova 2005; ellipsoidal, 15% axis jitter assumed).

## 6. Viewer features (`viewer/src/app.js`)

- Layers opened in place (after MouseBio_3D's zoom-unfold, `src/domain/zoom-unfold.ts` there): zooming in at a site opens the organ surface round the line going in; inside it the lobules stand cut across, with their own opening onto the duct tree of the lobules near the site; that opens onto a core of the tissue (the tested or generated 300 µm block, cut to a sphere of 145 µm radius with a flat top through its islet). Each opening grows by equal steps of zoom to 0.46 of the height of the view at the end of its range (MouseBio_3D's share) and no wider than the layer beneath; the openings of the lobules and the duct tree flare above their floors (slope 0.36) like a crater; each outer layer stays as a ring round the next and closes again as you zoom out. Ducts and vessels of the organ are not cut. Until the view is turned by hand it turns to look along the line; anything nearer than half the distance to the target is not drawn while the layers are open. The openings are fragment discards (`layered()` in `app.js`); names and picking check the same tests. Ranges (camera distance, µm): organ 45,000 to 14,000; lobules 12,000 to 3,200; core 2,600 to 1,000. Stops: lobules 4,200, tissue 800.
- Continuous zoom from organ to cell, with levels fading in and out by distance and the camera target drifting to the chosen structure; stages below the tissue (structure alone, its cells separated, one cell) hold with hysteresis so one can zoom out well before stepping back; Esc steps back.
- Three branches: acinus (with its intercalated duct and the ducts it drains into) → separated orange-segment acinar cells → acinar cell (rough ER, Golgi, zymogen granules, mitochondria); islet → separated islet cells → beta cell (insulin granules as dense cores in halos, primary cilium, beside a capillary); intralobular duct (with its collagen) → separated duct cells → duct cell (microvilli, primary cilium, tight junctions, CFTR and SLC26A6 markers, basement membrane).
- Islet cells at the cell level: beta, alpha and delta (ladder buttons; links beta-cell, alpha-cell, delta-cell), each the cell of that kind nearest the islet's middle cut, beside a short length of its nearest islet capillary. Granules after Brereton et al. 2015: beta about 350 nm with a dark core in a clear halo, alpha about 200 nm and dense with little or a grey halo, delta about 250 nm and homogeneous (not drawn larger; the paper's legend and text disagree). Delta cells reach their four nearest neighbours with processes (1 to 18 µm in human islets; Arrojo e Drigo et al. 2019). Alpha and delta cell size is not sourced (drawn as the beta cell).
- Secretion in motion (Motion on; the journey turns it on): acinar cell, granules travel from the Golgi to the apex, fuse, and their enzymes pass into the lumen and down the intercalated duct (Palade 1975; Messenger et al. 2014); acinus, enzymes leave along its ducts; duct cell, bicarbonate leaves through CFTR and SLC26A6 into the lumen and flows along it, and fluid runs along the duct (Lee et al. 2012); beta cell, glucose enters from the capillary and insulin granules fuse toward it, insulin leaving along the capillary (Rorsman and Ashcroft 2018); alpha cell, glucagon toward the capillary; delta cell, somatostatin at the ends of its processes. Paths, speeds and numbers are illustrative.
- Marker genes: a panel for the cell or structure in view, or the cell clicked, lists the HRA ASCT+B pancreas v1.3 biomarkers (and genes on related rows), and Human Protein Atlas v26.0 RNA markers with the basis on which each was chosen; genes link to HPA. Data in `repo/data/human-pancreas-markers.json` with sources, versions and checksums. HPA single-cell data has only acinar, duct and lumped islet cells, so delta and PP have only their hormone gene from HPA, and the endothelial, stellate and red-cell lists are cross-tissue.
- Modes (top left): Pancreas; Precursor lesions; Pancreatitis and PDA to come. Pancreas ladder: organ → tissue (both compartments) → exocrine (acinus, acinar cell, centroacinar cells; duct, duct cell) and endocrine (islet, its cells; beta, alpha, delta). Zooming still passes through the opened lobules on the way.
- Precursor lesions (link `#mode=precursors&lesion=panin|ipmn|mcn`): the organ, translucent, with PanIN (22 enlarged markers at the periphery of head and uncinate), MCN (5 cm cyst at the periphery of the tail), main-duct IPMN (the HRA main duct through the head, dilated, papillae inside) and branch-duct IPMN (grape-like cysts on a side branch near the head-neck junction), each joined by a line to a live inset of its view (after Hingorani 2007, Figure 1). The PanIN panel adds progression in numbers: Ki-67 index by grade (normal 0.41%, PanIN-1A 0.69%, 1B 2.33%, 2 14.08%, 3 22.01%, invasive 36.99%; Klein et al. 2002) and KRAS, MUC1 and cyclin D1 by grade (as reviewed in Koorstra et al. 2008), with a note that newer sequencing finds KRAS in over 90%. Clicking a lesion or inset, or zooming in on a lesion (the organ moves it to the middle; at 70 mm the view opens), opens its histology panel; Esc or "All precursors" returns. The panel shows real H&E micrographs (`viewer/assets/histology/`, credits and licences in CREDITS.md; embedded by build_viewer.py) with numbered pins placed by hand on each image (numbers only; the features are listed below and named on hover); each lesion also has a drawing in H&E colours generated in the page (`drawPanIN()`, `drawIPMN()`, `drawMCN()`, built from shared `lining()` and `papilla()` helpers: cells with height, mucin cap and nuclear position set per lesion and grade, among acini and stroma), shown first, with the real H&E below it carrying the same numbers, then links to more real examples on the Johns Hopkins pancreas pages (linked, not reproduced: not openly licensed); a list of the marked features, "what sets it apart", and a table comparing PanIN, IPMN and MCN (size, location, duct connection, lining, stroma, mucin, who, genes; Beger 4th ed. ch. 81, 86, 126; Hingorani 2007). PanIN: Hackeng et al. 2016 progression figure (normal → PanIN-1a/1b/2/3, with genetic changes). IPMN: Shibata et al. 2017 (gross specimen with main- and branch-duct IPMN; pancreatobiliary and gastric types). MCN: Nephron (very low power: normal pancreas, capsule, cyst, ovarian-type stroma) and Humpath (mucinous lining over ovarian-type stroma). The insets on the organ show the same images. (The 3D sketches of the lesions were replaced by the histology.)
  Cards per lesion (where, size, description, grades, genes, demographics, survival) cite Hingorani 2007, Rodriguez 2007, Beger 4th ed. and Robbins 9th ed. The cancer (PDA) map and card remain in the code for the PDA mode.
- Thirty tissue sites (10 per region, scattered at random; each shows its region's tested block, so switching is instant; sites in the uncinate process get a PP-rich islet: PP 60%, beta 30%, alpha 5%, delta 5% of cells, after the 54–94% PP islet volume reported there, Gorelick and Williams 2021 ch. 1; the split is assumed), not shown: clicking the organ, or starting to zoom in on it from afar, picks one of the four sites nearest the point under the pointer at random and brings it to the middle of the view; 25 acini and 25 ducts per block chosen by farthest-point sampling; islets differ per site; pickers step through them.
- Stain: H&E (default; hematoxylin purple bases, eosin pink apices, red granules, pale islets with uniform nuclei, pink collagen; cut cells filled with their stain; stain-like grain) or Cell types (multiplex-immunofluorescence colours on a dark ground: acinar orange, duct teal, beta green, alpha magenta, delta cyan, PP amber, capillaries red, stellate yellow, collagen lavender, nuclei blue).
- Cut open (off by default): a cutaway facing the viewer through the point being zoomed into (ducts opened lengthwise).
- Slice apart: the organ into eight transverse slices, the tissue core into six sections across the view (each slab keeps the core's shape; the lobules fade), and a structure or a single cell into five; spacing knob. The surfaces are hollow, so each slab draws the inside of a cut as flat, solid tissue (back faces shaded), and organ slabs are opaque. Moving particles are left out of slices.
- Split screen: two independent views; presets Islet | Acinus, Islet | Duct, Acinus | Duct, Beta cell | Acinar cell, Tissue | Cell, Organ | Tissue.
- Play the journey: 13 narrated steps (text paraphrased from StatPearls and histology teaching sources), Google UK English voices only (Female by default, Male offered); browsers without them fall back to standard UK narrators (Microsoft Sonia, Libby, Ryan; Apple Daniel, Serena, Kate), adjustable speed (0.88× default), sentence-by-sentence pacing; continues while the user interacts; Stop ends it.
- Names on structures, click to identify with isolate and opacity, illustrative red cell flow, scale bar, regions and compare view, faint surroundings option.
- Links to a view: the address follows the view (`#site=12&view=islet&stain=if`; views organ, lobules, tissue, acinus, acinus-cells, acinar-cell, islet, islet-cells, beta-cell, duct, duct-cells, duct-cell); opening such a link, or changing the hash, flies there. Names behind the solid lobules (when the view is turned inside the opening) are hidden by testing the line of sight against the same column and hole as the shader.

## 7. Acceptance checks

- `npx tsx --test tests/human-pancreas.test.ts`: 23 tests (inventory, registries, extraction, simulated vessels and nodes, islet model and distribution, tissue blocks per region, generators, ontology).
- Full MouseBio_3D suite passes with the module (699 at handover).
- `viewer/build_viewer.py` reproduces `dist/` byte for byte from sources; `viewer/test/smoke.py` loads the page, zooms in over the organ, walks the layer stops, every branch down to its cells, the cell-type stain, the comparison and a link to a view, and fails on any page error or missed stage. GitHub Actions runs it (software WebGL) before each Pages deployment.
